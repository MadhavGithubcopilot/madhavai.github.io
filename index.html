02 / LEADERSHIP DNA

Leadership is a practice.

Being a founder is not just having an idea. It is learning to make thoughtful decisions and take responsibility for the result.

CAPABILITY / 01

Strategic thinking

Break a big vision into priorities. Compare options, understand trade-offs and choose the next useful step.

VISION → PRIORITIES → ACTION
CAPABILITY / 02

Communication

Explain complex ideas simply, present with confidence, listen carefully and adapt the message to the audience.

CLARITY BUILDS TRUST
CAPABILITY / 03

Decision-making

Separate facts from assumptions, compare alternatives and explain why a decision makes sense.

EVIDENCE OVER EGO
CAPABILITY / 04

Product thinking

Start with a real user problem. Test whether a solution is useful before spending time making it bigger.

PROBLEM BEFORE PRODUCT
CAPABILITY / 05

Team leadership

Set clear expectations, respect different viewpoints, share credit and take responsibility when things go wrong.

ACCOUNTABILITY MATTERS
CAPABILITY / 06

Business awareness

Think about customer needs, costs, value, resources, competition and whether an idea can work in practice.

05 / FOUNDER'S JOURNAL

Reflect. Learn. Improve.

Leadership develops through reflection and follow-through. Use this journal to document your thinking and learn from real project experiences.

PRINCIPLE / 01

Progress beats the perfect plan.

A plan is useful, but evidence comes from action. Build a small version, test it and use what you learn to choose the next step.

ASK YOURSELF: What can I test this week?
PRINCIPLE / 02

Listen before you lead.

Leadership is not about having the loudest voice. Understand the problem, listen to different perspectives and communicate your reasoning.

ASK YOURSELF: Whose perspective am I missing?
PRINCIPLE / 03

Be honest about the evidence.

A prototype is not a finished product, and an assumption is not a proven result. Document what works, what fails and what remains unknown.

ASK YOURSELF: What have I actually verified?
